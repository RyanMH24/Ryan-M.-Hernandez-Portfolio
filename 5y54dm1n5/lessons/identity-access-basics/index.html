Identity & Access Fundamentals

SSO, what a directory service actually is, and the user provisioning/deprovisioning lifecycle.

SSO, one level deeper

You've already met SSO: log in once, reach every system you're authorized for. The piece that makes it work is an identity provider — a central service that authenticates the user once and then vouches for them to every other connected app, instead of each app checking a password itself. Products like Okta exist to be that identity provider; the concept matters more than any specific vendor's name for it.

The directory: where identity actually lives

An identity provider still needs to know who the user is — their name, their email, what team they're on, what groups they belong to. That's the job of a directory service. On a Windows-centric network that's AD (Active Directory); Microsoft's cloud equivalent is Entra ID (formerly Azure AD). Either way, the directory is the source of truth: when someone's title changes or they leave the company, you update it in the directory, and everything downstream — SSO, app access, group policy — is supposed to follow from that one update.

The user lifecycle: provisioning to deprovisioning

When someone joins, an admin (or an automated workflow) provisions their account: creates it in the directory, assigns them to the right groups, and grants access to the apps their role needs. When someone leaves, the reverse has to happen — deprovisioning: deactivate the account, revoke app access, and make sure nothing is left reachable with credentials that shouldn't work anymore. Deprovisioning promptly matters as much as provisioning correctly; a former employee's still-active account is a real security gap, not a theoretical one.

Why this matters day to day

Most identity tickets fall into one of these buckets: someone can't get into something they should have access to (a provisioning or group membership problem), someone has access they shouldn't (a deprovisioning gap), or SSO itself is misbehaving (the identity provider can't reach an app, or vice versa). Knowing which bucket a ticket falls into is most of the battle before you even open an admin console.

Practice it

Reading about the user lifecycle only gets you so far — try provisioning and deactivating a real account in the Identity Console scenario.
