Sysadmin Terminology

A tour of the acronyms and terms you'll hear constantly in IT support and administration.

Why a whole lesson on vocabulary

Walk into almost any IT support role and you'll hear a wall of acronyms on day one, used as if everyone already knows them. This lesson is a fast tour of the terms that come up most — the full reference lives in the glossary if you ever need to look one up again later.

Identity and access

SSO (Single Sign-On) lets someone log in once and reach every system they're authorized for, instead of juggling a dozen separate passwords. The system that holds those identities — who exists, what group they're in, what they're allowed to touch — is often AD (Active Directory) on a Windows-based network. AD applies rules to users and computers through GPOs (Group Policy Objects): a GPO might enforce a minimum password length, deploy software, or lock a desktop background across an entire department at once.

Managing devices

MDM (Mobile Device Management) is the device-side counterpart to AD — it enrolls, configures, secures, and can remotely wipe laptops and phones. Every device MDM manages is an endpoint: company terminology for "a thing that connects to the network," whether that's a laptop, a phone, or a server.

Support and service terms

When someone reports a problem, it becomes a ticket in a ticket queue — the ordered backlog of open issues waiting to be worked. If the first person who picks it up can't resolve it, they perform an escalation: handing it to a more senior or specialized team rather than letting it sit unresolved. How fast a ticket is supposed to be answered and fixed is usually governed by an SLA (Service Level Agreement) — a commitment the support team has made about response and resolution time.

Putting it together

None of these terms are complicated on their own — the hard part is just that they all get thrown around at once, in conversation, without definition. Once you've got SSO, AD, GPO, MDM, endpoint, ticket queue, escalation, and SLA solid, most day-one conversations in an IT support role stop sounding like a different language.
